Unit 2 • Crime Scene Investigation

Every scene tells a story. Your job is to protect it.

In this unit, you will learn how investigators secure a crime scene, document what they see, collect evidence carefully, and protect the chain of custody so evidence can still be trusted later.

Student Notes

Click through the tabs. These are the ideas you should understand well enough to explain in your own words.

1️⃣
A crime scene is any location where evidence may be found.
It may be the main location, a secondary location, a vehicle, a digital device, or even a person’s clothing.
2️⃣
The first priority is safety.
Investigators check for danger, help injured people, and prevent more harm before collecting evidence.
3️⃣
Once safe, the scene must be protected.
Too many people walking through a scene can move, destroy, or contaminate evidence.
🔐
Secure the scene — create boundaries and control entry.
👥
Separate witnesses — avoid story sharing or accidental influence.
🔎
Scan the scene — look for obvious evidence and safety concerns.
📸
See/photograph the scene — record before touching or moving evidence.
✏️
Sketch the scene — show locations, measurements, and relationships.
🔍
Search for evidence — use an organized pattern so evidence is not missed.
🧪
Secure and collect evidence — package, label, and document each item.
🧬
Biological evidence comes from living things: blood, saliva, hair with tissue, skin cells, or plant material.
🧵
Trace evidence is small material transferred during contact: fibers, glass, soil, paint, pollen, or gunshot residue.
🔨
Physical evidence is any object or material that may connect people, places, or events.
⚠️
Contamination happens when evidence is changed, mixed, or exposed to something that makes it less reliable.
🧾
Chain of custody is the documented history of evidence.
It shows who collected it, who handled it, when it moved, and where it was stored.
⚖️
If the chain is broken, evidence can be challenged.
A court must trust that the item tested is the same item collected at the scene.
✅
Good evidence records include: case number, item number, description, date/time, location, collector, and transfers.
