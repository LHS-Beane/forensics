Unit 3 • Hair Analysis

A single hair can tell part of a story.

In this unit, you will examine hair as trace evidence. You will learn what investigators can observe under a microscope, what hair evidence can and cannot prove, and how careful measurement helps compare samples.

General Notes

Use the tabs to review the main ideas. These notes are meant to help you complete your packet and prepare for labs.

🔁
Hair is trace evidence.
It can transfer from person to person, person to object, or object to person through contact.
🧪
Hair can help investigators compare samples.
Color, thickness, medulla pattern, cuticle pattern, and treatment damage can help decide whether samples are consistent.
⚠️
Consistent does not mean identical.
A microscopic match can support an investigation, but it normally cannot prove a hair came from one exact person.

Cuticle

The outer layer of overlapping scales. Scale patterns can be observed under magnification.

Cortex

The thick middle layer containing pigment. It affects color and visible texture.

Medulla

The central core. It may be continuous, interrupted, fragmented, or absent.

🧠
Think of hair like a pencil.
Outer coating = cuticle, wooden body = cortex, center lead = medulla.
🔬
Microscopes reveal features too small to see clearly with the naked eye.
Students compare color, medulla, cuticle, curl/shape, width, and unusual characteristics.
📐
Field of view helps estimate size.
If you know the diameter of the circular field you see, you can estimate the width of a hair in microns.
✏️
Sketches matter.
A good sketch shows the feature you observed, not just a random line.
⚖️
Hair comparison has limits.
Microscopic hair comparison is better for including, excluding, or supporting a possibility than proving identity by itself.
🧬
DNA can make hair evidence stronger.
Hair roots may contain nuclear DNA. Hair shafts may sometimes be tested using mitochondrial DNA or newer methods, depending on the sample.
✅
Best claim wording:
“This hair is consistent with the suspect sample,” not “this hair definitely came from the suspect.”

Course image: a ruler viewed under microscope magnification. Use visuals like this to understand field of view and how tiny measurements are estimated.

📏
1 millimeter = 1000 microns.
Microns are useful because hair is much smaller than a full millimeter.

Medullary Index Calculator

The medullary index compares the width of the medulla to the width of the entire hair shaft.

How to Use the Number

➗
Formula: medullary index = medulla width ÷ hair shaft width.
🧪
Compare, do not overclaim.
The number is one characteristic. You still need color, cuticle pattern, medulla pattern, width, and other observations.
✍️
Notebook sentence frame:
“The questioned hair is consistent with Sample ___ because ___, ___, and ___.”

Quick Check Questions

Because many people can share similar hair characteristics. Without stronger testing, hair usually supports a category or comparison rather than proving one unique source.
It means the sample shares important characteristics with another sample. It does not automatically mean both came from the same person.
Hair is very small. Microns allow you to describe tiny measurements more clearly than fractions of a millimeter.
Sketches force you to slow down, notice details, and create a record of what you actually observed.
